## How provenance is handled going forward

**Nothing enters this library without a known origin.** Every prompt is either written by us or
generated by us using a commercial AI service under terms that assign output ownership to the
customer. We do not adapt third-party publications.

**Primary law is cited, never reproduced.** Statutes, rules of court, and practice directions are
referred to by number so you can read the authoritative version. We do not paste their text, and
we do not reproduce judgments, headnotes, or commentary from subscription databases.

**Third-party frameworks are not borrowed.** Where a prompt technique is genuinely common
knowledge — source grounding, chain-of-thought, specifying output format, verification — we say it
in our own words. Ideas and methods are not anyone's property; specific wording is.

**Every release is scanned.** `tools/check_overlap.py` compares the library against a blocklist of
every third-party publication we have ever consulted, using 8-gram shingle analysis, and fails on
any run of more than 15 consecutive verbatim words. It measures wording rather than topic, because
copyright protects expression and two prompts on the same subject will legitimately share
vocabulary. This check is what would have caught the problem above, and it now runs before release.

The blocklist itself is deliberately not committed to this repository — those files are other
people's copyrighted work, and committing them would recreate a smaller version of the same
mistake.
